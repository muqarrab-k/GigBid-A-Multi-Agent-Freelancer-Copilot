# Grant impact report for Riverbend Education Fund

In March and April 2024, I worked with Riverbend Education Fund on a fixed-scope project to support a grant renewal. The nonprofit needed a clear, credible way to show how the funded work was affecting student outcomes. Their internal data was available, but it was not yet packaged into a concise report that decision-makers could scan quickly and use confidently during the renewal process.

My focus was to turn the available attendance and test-score data into a short impact report that was easy to understand and grounded in evidence. I used Excel to organize and review the source data, applied statistics to summarize the changes, and built charts in Tableau to make the trends visible at a glance. I structured the report so the most important findings came first, followed by the supporting visuals and concise interpretation.

A key part of the work was keeping the report tight and persuasive. Rather than overwhelming the reader with raw tables, I selected only the measures that spoke directly to program impact: attendance patterns and test-score changes. That allowed Riverbend Education Fund to present a simple narrative supported by clear charts and measured analysis.

```text
Metric: attendance change
Metric: test-score change
View: grant renewal summary
Chart type: line and bar
Output: short impact report
Audience: renewal reviewers
```

The final deliverable gave Riverbend Education Fund a polished report they could use in the grant renewal process. The outcome was tangible: the report supported a $1.2M grant renewal that was approved. For me, that was the clearest sign that the work did its job—helping the organization communicate impact in a way that was both understandable and credible.
